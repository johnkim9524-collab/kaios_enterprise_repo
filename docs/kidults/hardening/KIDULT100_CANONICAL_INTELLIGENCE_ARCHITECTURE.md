# KIDULT 100 Canonical Intelligence Architecture

Status: CANONICAL WORKING CONTRACT
Branch: hardening/p0-speed-quality
Objective: define the value, universe, category model, source taxonomy, data policy, vertical intelligence architecture, WHY system, capacity requirements, and operating gates required for KIDULTS to become a category-defining global collectibles intelligence benchmark.

## 1. Prime directive

KIDULTS does not collect the world. It collects the signals that explain what matters, why it matters, what it is worth, what should be done now, and what may matter next.

Canonical value sequence:

CUSTOMER CURIOSITY -> DECISION VALUE -> INTELLIGENCE QUESTION -> REQUIRED SIGNAL -> REQUIRED DATA -> LEGAL SOURCE -> RIGHTS/PREFLIGHT -> COLLECTION -> NORMALIZATION -> INTELLIGENCE -> WHY -> DECISION -> EVIDENCE -> HISTORY

Data availability never defines the product. Intelligence value defines the data requirement.

## 2. Core customer value

KIDULTS must create value for collectors, institutions, advisors, auction specialists, insurers, wealth managers, brands, archives, researchers and professional market participants.

The canonical customer values are:

1. DISCOVER — identify important objects before consensus.
2. WHY — explain why the object matters and why its status is changing.
3. VALUE — determine fair value, relative value and comparable context.
4. TIMING — determine whether the market state is attractive, overheated, weakening or re-entering.
5. CONVICTION — provide evidence-backed buy/watch/hold/avoid confidence.
6. RELATIVE VALUE — compare alternatives across and within categories.
7. SCARCITY — determine structural, effective and investable scarcity.
8. LIQUIDITY — determine whether an object can actually transact and at what friction/risk.
9. CANON — determine cultural, design, historical and institutional importance.
10. RISK — expose authenticity, condition, concentration, hype, provenance, data and market-structure risk.
11. PORTFOLIO INTELLIGENCE — identify collection gaps, concentration, missing icons, liquidity profile and canon exposure.
12. FUTURE CANON — estimate the probability that an object becomes more culturally and institutionally important over time.

## 3. WHY is mandatory

Every material ranking, score, valuation, confidence or state change must support:

WHAT changed -> WHY -> supporting evidence -> counter-evidence -> confidence -> SO WHAT -> WHAT NEXT -> invalidation condition.

A rank movement without WHY is not a decision-grade KIDULTS output.

The long-term moat is a longitudinal WHY Graph:

Signal -> Cause Candidate -> Supporting Evidence -> Counter Evidence -> Outcome -> Confidence History.

## 4. Kidult 100 universe

Published index size: 100.

Candidate universe requirements:
- absolute minimum: 300 candidates.
- operating target: 400–500 candidates.
- future extensibility: 1,000+ observable candidate objects without changing the core model.

Selection pipeline:

300–500 Candidate Universe -> identity/provenance eligibility -> rights eligibility -> data sufficiency -> vertical factor eligibility -> WHY eligibility -> risk/confidence gate -> cross-category normalization -> Kidult 100.

The Kidult 100 is not a list of the most expensive objects. It is the global benchmark for objects shaping collectible culture today and tomorrow.

## 5. Category model

Eight Core Categories are the current operating taxonomy, not a permanent closed universe:

1. Toys & Models
2. Watches & Jewelry
3. Automobiles & Mobility
4. Fashion & Accessories
5. Design & Furniture
6. Technology & Cameras
7. Gaming / Music / Screen Culture
8. Cards / Comics / Memorabilia

Expansion model: 8 Core + Dynamic Expansion.

A new category may be added when it demonstrates:
- cultural significance,
- persistent collector behavior,
- structural scarcity,
- observable secondary market,
- sufficient data observability,
- cross-generation persistence.

The Level-1 universe definition is: objects collected for cultural meaning, identity, scarcity, design, history or passion.

Level-2 contains Core Categories. Level-3 contains dynamic subcategories and may evolve continuously.

Index diversification must be constraint-based rather than quota-based. Do not force equal category weights. Use minimum representation and maximum concentration constraints while preserving merit-based ranking.

## 6. Universal Intelligence Core

Every candidate object is evaluated under a common contract:

Identity / Provenance / WHY / Value / Scarcity / Liquidity / Demand-Momentum / Canon / Risk-Confidence / Future.

This common layer makes cross-category comparison possible.

## 7. Vertical Intelligence DNA

Category-specific markets must not be forced into one raw-data model. Use a Common Core + Vertical Intelligence architecture.

### Toys & Models
Typical DNA: edition/population, sealed/completeness, condition, franchise longevity, production discontinuation, collector depth, grading population.

### Watches & Jewelry
Typical DNA: reference, variation, movement, serial range, dial/case configuration, condition/polishing, completeness, production scarcity, provenance.

### Automobiles & Mobility
Typical DNA: production count, chassis/specification, originality, mileage, accident/restoration history, ownership/provenance, configuration, concours recognition, transaction history.

### Fashion & Accessories
Typical DNA: designer, era/season, runway significance, production, archive rarity, celebrity/cultural adoption, institutionalization, resale depth.

### Design & Furniture
Typical DNA: designer, edition, manufacturer, production history, provenance, condition, gallery/auction history, museum/canon recognition.

### Technology & Cameras
Typical DNA: generation, manufacturer history, serial/production range, technical/design significance, originality/completeness, enthusiast persistence.

### Gaming / Music / Screen Culture
Typical DNA: first edition/release, publisher/studio/creator provenance, franchise significance, format/variant, cultural reach, grading/condition, historical importance.

### Cards / Comics / Memorabilia
Typical DNA: print/run, grade population, issue/rookie/event significance, player/character canon, provenance, authentication, historical event relevance, transaction depth.

Each future vertical must register its own Intelligence DNA, source graph, factor model, WHY causal model and normalization rules before activation.

## 8. Source-channel taxonomy

Sources are classified by their function in the lifecycle of an object and market, not merely by API type.

S1 Manufacturer / Brand — official product identity, MSRP, edition, SKU/reference, production and discontinuation facts.
S2 Designer / Creator / IP Owner — creator, collaboration, archive, provenance and cultural significance.
S3 Primary Retail / Authorized Distribution — retail pricing, availability, allocation, stock and channel state.
S4 Secondary Marketplace — listing price, inventory, velocity, seller depth and secondary-market state.
S5 Auction House — estimate, hammer, premium, lot frequency, sell-through and transaction comparables.
S6 Dealer / Specialist Broker — specialist inventory, asking market, condition, configuration and market commentary.
S7 Authentication / Grading / Registry — authentication, grade, population, serial/registry and certification evidence.
S8 Institution / Museum / Archive — acquisition, exhibition, collection inclusion, scholarly/institutional recognition.
S9 Community / Collector Network — collector discussion, ownership/desire signals, specialist knowledge and emerging demand.
S10 Search / Social / Attention — search interest, attention velocity and cross-platform cultural momentum.
S11 News / Magazine / Editorial — narrative formation, critical recognition, cultural context and canon shift.
S12 Reference / Research / Government / Public Data — historical facts, public records, research and independent verification.
S13 KIDULTS Derived Intelligence — entity graph, comparable graph, scarcity history, liquidity history, WHY graph, confidence history, Future Canon, index-decision history.

## 9. Source authority tiers

Tier A Authoritative Truth: manufacturer, registry, museum/archive, official/government record. Best for identity, production, authenticity and institutional recognition.

Tier B Transaction Truth: auction, verified sold-market data, executed transaction feeds. Best for price, comparable and liquidity evidence.

Tier C Market State: retail, reseller inventory, dealer listings and supply observations. Best for availability, asking structure and supply pressure.

Tier D Demand & Culture: community, search, social, editorial and media. Best for early attention, narrative and cultural momentum.

No single tier is sufficient for WHY. KIDULTS creates value by triangulating independent tiers.

## 10. Source Class × Vertical matrix

Source management is two-dimensional. A source record must include:
- source class,
- vertical/category coverage,
- geography,
- allowed data types,
- authority level,
- legal/rights status,
- permitted collection method,
- refresh frequency,
- historical depth,
- reliability/SLA where applicable,
- intelligence contribution,
- fallback/verification source,
- retention and derived-data rights.

The same source class may have completely different high-value providers by vertical. Auction sources for automobiles, watches, design and memorabilia must therefore be maintained as vertical source graphs rather than one global auction bucket.

## 11. Data primitives — collect only decision-grade signals

KIDULTS collects seven core intelligence primitives:

1. Identity & Provenance
2. Scarcity & Supply
3. Transaction / Price / Comparable
4. Liquidity
5. Demand / Attention
6. Canon / Institutional / Cultural Recognition
7. Risk / Confidence

If a field does not materially improve at least one defined Intelligence value, it should normally not be collected.

Low-priority examples: copied product descriptions, generic brand biographies, redundant specifications, meaningless mention counts, unlinked metadata.

High-priority examples: actual transaction, supply change, listing duration, sell-through, auction frequency, grade population, institutional acquisition/exhibition, search/attention change, scarcity events, creator/brand canon events, cross-market comparable changes.

Time series are strategically preferred over static metadata because longitudinal history becomes proprietary intelligence capital.

## 12. Rights-first collection gate

Before a source becomes active it must pass:

INTELLIGENCE VALUE -> LEGAL BASIS -> ALLOWED FIELDS -> ALLOWED COLLECTION METHOD -> REFRESH/RATE -> PROVENANCE -> VOLUME/QUALITY -> ECONOMICS -> FALLBACK -> RETENTION/DERIVED RIGHTS.

Publicly visible does not automatically mean automated collection or commercial reuse is permitted. Unclear rights status means HOLD, not ASSUME.

## 13. Data quality target

Index-grade targets:
- canonical entity resolution >= 99.5%,
- critical provenance = 100%,
- rights classification = 100%,
- duplicate contamination < 0.5%,
- critical stale/invalid rejection = 100%,
- asking vs sold classification = 100%,
- currency/time normalization = 100%,
- material rank/score changes with WHY = 100%,
- derived signal confidence attached = 100%.

Objects failing minimum evidence or confidence must be excluded or explicitly held rather than filled with invented estimates.

## 14. Volume and capacity philosophy

The platform optimizes for Decision-Grade Observations, not raw-record vanity volume.

Initial 300-object universe planning range:
- 5–20 material observations per object per month,
- approximately 1,500–6,000 decision-grade observations/month,
- approximately 18,000–72,000/year before high-frequency market feeds.

With transaction, listing and attention feeds, architecture must scale toward hundreds of thousands to several million observations/year while retaining provenance and deterministic replay.

## 15. Canonical processing pipeline

INGEST -> RIGHTS PREFLIGHT -> NORMALIZE -> ENTITY RESOLUTION -> DEDUP -> PROVENANCE -> QUALITY -> TIME SERIES -> VERTICAL SIGNALS -> COMPARABLE GRAPH -> WHY GRAPH -> VERTICAL SCORE -> CROSS-CATEGORY NORMALIZATION -> KIDULT 100 -> MONITOR -> RECOVERY.

## 16. Two-stage scoring

Stage 1: Vertical Score. Each category uses its own domain-relevant factors and weights.

Stage 2: Cross-Category Normalization. Vertical scores are normalized into the common KIDULTS universe so an automobile, watch, design object and collectible toy can be compared without pretending they share identical raw drivers.

A universal raw-weight model is prohibited unless evidence demonstrates that it preserves category validity.

## 18. Strategic moat

Raw data may be purchasable by competitors. The intended moat is the compounding combination of:
- Historical Observation Series,
- Canonical Entity Graph,
- Comparable Graph,
- rights-aware provenance history,
- category-specific Vertical Intelligence DNA,
- cross-category normalization methodology,
- WHY and counter-evidence graph,

- scarcity/liquidity/demand/canon lead-lag history,

The target is not to know the most facts. The target is to make the most defensible, explainable and timely decisions about collectible culture.
