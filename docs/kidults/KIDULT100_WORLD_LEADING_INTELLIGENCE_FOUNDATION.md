# KIDULT 100 — World-Leading Intelligence Foundation

Status: GOVERNING DESIGN BASELINE
Program: KIDULTS 120 / Issue #210
Principle: VALUE BEFORE DATA. SPEED × QUALITY.

## 1. Mission
KIDULTS does not win by collecting the most data. It wins by identifying the smallest set of longitudinal, legally usable, provenance-backed signals that best answer high-value collector and institutional questions.

Core promise:
**KIDULTS identifies what matters, explains WHY it matters, quantifies what it may be worth, supports what to do now, and anticipates what may matter next.**

Canonical intelligence loop:
**WHAT → WHY → SO WHAT → WHAT NEXT → EVIDENCE → CONFIDENCE**

Kidult 100 is the flagship benchmark: the 100 objects that best represent collectible culture today and its likely future canon. It is selected from a minimum 3× candidate universe; target operating universe is 300–500 candidates, never a fixed list of only 100.

## 2. Irreplaceable customer value
Every product, signal, source and field must improve at least one of these values:
1. DISCOVERY — find important objects before broad consensus.
2. WHY — explain why an object, category or market state matters or changed.
3. VALUE — establish defensible relative/fair-value ranges and comparables.
4. TIMING — identify accumulation, watch, overheating, re-entry and regime-change states.
5. CONVICTION — combine evidence, counter-evidence and confidence into decision support.
6. RELATIVE VALUE — answer A vs B, category vs category, and object vs comparable-set questions.
7. SCARCITY — distinguish claimed rarity from observable effective scarcity.
8. LIQUIDITY — estimate ability to transact, depth, frequency and exit risk.
9. CANON — measure durable cultural, design and institutional significance.
10. RISK — expose provenance, authenticity, concentration, manipulation, condition and data-confidence risk.
11. PORTFOLIO INTELLIGENCE — identify collection concentration, gaps, missing icons and emerging exposure.
12. FUTURE CANON — estimate probability that an object becomes more culturally/institutionally important over 3–10 years.

No-data rule: if a field does not materially improve one of the above, do not collect it by default.

## 3. Candidate universe — minimum 3×
### Required operating size
- Hard minimum: 300 candidates.
- Preferred steady-state: 400–500 candidates.
- Published benchmark: 100 constituents.
- Watchlist: next 100–150.
- Discovery pool: remainder.

### Candidate families
The universe is object-led, not toy-led. Initial families:
- Toys, figures, model kits, construction systems and designer toys
- Watches and wearable mechanical/design icons
- Automobiles, motorcycles and mobility design icons
- Sneakers, bags, footwear and fashion objects with durable collector markets
- Cameras, audio, computing and consumer-technology design icons
- Furniture, industrial design and household design objects
- Entertainment, gaming, music and screen-culture artifacts where rights/data permit
- Cards, comics, printed objects and memorabilia where reliable market/provenance evidence exists

### Candidate admission test
A candidate enters the 300+ universe only if it has:
- stable canonical identity;
- evidence of collector relevance or emerging collector relevance;
- at least one lawful authoritative/primary or high-quality reference channel;
- a plausible path to at least 4 of the 7 core intelligence primitives below;
- sufficient persistence to support longitudinal tracking;
- no known legal/rights barrier that makes the intended data use untenable.

### Core intelligence primitives
1. Identity / provenance
2. Scarcity / supply state
3. Transaction / price / comparable state
4. Liquidity / market depth
5. Demand / attention / community state
6. Canon / institutional / cultural state
7. Risk / confidence / counter-evidence

Full Kidult 100 eligibility should ultimately require strong coverage of identity/provenance + scarcity + market/comparables + canon + risk, with liquidity and demand measured where lawful and reliable rather than fabricated.

## 4. Data characteristics required to create a moat
Priority is not metadata breadth; it is decision usefulness and time-series compounding.

### A. Identity and provenance
Collect: canonical name/model/reference, maker/brand/designer, release/production period, edition/reference identifiers, authoritative source IDs, provenance source, observation time, payload/content fingerprint, rights class.
Creates: entity graph, cross-source joins, provenance confidence, comparable integrity.

### B. Scarcity and supply
Collect: edition/production quantity when authoritative, production status, discontinued state, observed listings/supply counts where permitted, availability changes, surviving/known examples when credible, institutional holdings.
Creates: effective scarcity, supply compression, scarcity change, false-rarity detection.

### C. Transaction, price and comparables
Collect only where lawful: realized transaction price, date, currency, venue, condition/grade, fees treatment, estimate vs realized, asking vs sold distinction, comparable identity, repeat-sale observations.
Creates: fair-value bands, comparable curves, dispersion, price regimes, relative value.

### D. Liquidity
Collect: transaction frequency, time between observed trades, sell-through where available, bid/offer/listing depth where permitted, venue diversity, concentration.
Creates: liquidity score, exit risk, market depth, confidence in valuation.

### E. Demand and attention
Collect only meaningful signals: search trend deltas, watch/follow/save/bid counts where legally accessible, community growth/engagement quality, cross-region attention, waitlist or sold-out transitions, auction participation where available.
Creates: demand momentum, attention-to-price lead/lag, emerging-icon detection.

### F. Canon and cultural/institutional strength
Collect: museum/institution holdings, exhibitions, design awards, authoritative publications/catalogues, retrospectives, creator significance, major cultural references, anniversary/reissue events, institutional acquisitions.
Creates: Canon Strength, institutional relevance, persistence, Future Canon Probability.

### G. Risk and confidence
Collect: source conflicts, provenance gaps, authentication/condition evidence where available, price dispersion, thin-market flags, concentration, suspected manipulation/anomalies, legal/rights uncertainty, stale data.
Creates: confidence score, risk flags, reasoned uncertainty.

## 5. WHY engine — mandatory differentiation
KIDULTS must never publish a material rank/score movement without an evidence-backed explanation.

For every material movement, store:
- WHAT changed;
- candidate causal signals;
- supporting evidence;
- counter-evidence;
- time relationship / lead-lag;
- confidence;
- alternative explanation;
- SO WHAT for value/risk/collector/institution;
- WHAT NEXT: observable catalysts and invalidation conditions.

The platform must distinguish correlation from demonstrated causation. Where causality is not established, language must remain probabilistic.

## 6. Source-channel universe and legal tiers
This is a source architecture, not a blanket permission claim. Every source requires source-specific terms/licence/robots/API review before activation.

### Tier 0 — First-party / user-contributed with explicit rights
Examples: KIDULTS observations, opt-in collector portfolios, institution/brand submissions, contracted feeds.
Use: highest strategic value when rights and provenance are explicit.

### Tier 1 — Open-access / public APIs with explicit reusable-data terms
Examples already under operational evaluation: The Metropolitan Museum of Art Open Access API; Art Institute of Chicago API/Open Access; Smithsonian Open Access/API candidate.
Use: identity, canon, institutional presence, creator/object metadata, provenance anchors.

### Tier 2 — Government / public-domain / open-licence datasets
Examples: cultural heritage/open-data portals, trademark/patent/public registration datasets where relevant and terms permit.
Use: identity, legal status, historical/contextual events.

### Tier 3 — Official brand/manufacturer/institution feeds and pages
Use only when terms/API/permission support the intended automated use. Do not assume public visibility equals permission for automated collection.
Use: reference identity, release/discontinuation, specifications, authoritative events.

### Tier 4 — Auction houses, marketplaces, dealers, grading/authentication and resale venues
Highest market-intelligence value, but rights vary materially. Prefer official APIs, feeds, licensed datasets, affiliate/data programmes, public results with explicit permitted reuse, or contracts.
Use: transactions, price, liquidity, supply, condition, comparables.

### Tier 5 — Search/trend/community/social channels
Use official APIs, aggregate trend products, licensed analytics or terms-compliant public datasets. Avoid uncontrolled scraping of restricted personal/community data.
Use: attention, demand, regional momentum, narrative emergence.

### Tier 6 — Editorial, research and cultural publications
Use metadata/facts/links/licensed text as rights permit; avoid copying protected expression.
Use: canon events, cultural context, creator/brand significance, event detection.

### Tier 7 — Derived KIDULTS proprietary data
Historical normalized observations, entity links, comparable sets, factor histories, WHY graphs, confidence histories, category rotations, index decisions.
This is the core compounding asset and must preserve lineage back to source evidence and rights class.

## 7. Source activation gate
A channel is activated only after:
1. Intelligence value mapped.
2. Legal basis/terms/licence recorded.
3. Allowed fields and prohibited fields recorded.
4. Collection method allowed (API/feed/download/manual/contracted/etc.).
5. Refresh/rate limits known.
6. Provenance requirements defined.
7. Expected volume and quality profiled.
8. Cost and operational burden acceptable.
9. Failure/fallback behavior defined.
10. Retention/derived-data rights understood.

If uncertain: HOLD, not scrape.

## 8. Data volume and quality model
We do not optimize for raw volume. We optimize for **decision-grade observations per constituent per unit time**.

### Initial operating target for 300 candidates
- 300 canonical entities minimum.
- 5–20 high-value observations/entity/month initially depending on category/source availability.
- Approx. 1,500–6,000 decision-grade observations/month at launch.
- 18,000–72,000 observations/year before market-feed expansion.
- With transactions/listings/attention feeds: scale may reach 0.5–5M observations/year; architecture must handle this without changing the canonical model.

### Quality targets
- canonical identity resolution ≥99.5% for index-eligible entities;
- critical provenance coverage 100%;
- rights classification 100%;
- duplicate contamination <0.5%;
- stale/invalid critical observations rejected 100% in test set;
- transaction asking/sold distinction 100%;
- currency/time normalization 100%;
- material score/rank movement with WHY evidence 100%;
- source conflict surfaced, never silently overwritten;
- confidence attached to every derived material signal.

## 9. Platform processing capacity readiness
The platform must be ready for the data model before expanding collection.

Required pipeline:
INGEST → RIGHTS/PREFLIGHT → NORMALIZE → ENTITY RESOLUTION → DEDUP → PROVENANCE → QUALITY → TIME-SERIES STORE → SIGNAL EXTRACTION → COMPARABLE GRAPH → WHY GRAPH → FACTOR SCORES → KIDULT100 DECISION → POST-PUBLISH MONITOR → RECOVERY.

### Capacity gates
Phase 1: 300 candidates / <100k observations/year — full provenance and deterministic replay.
Phase 2: 1,000 candidates / 0.5–5M observations/year — partition/index stress, provider concurrency, incremental recompute.
Phase 3: multi-market institutional service — source isolation, event streaming/batching as justified, SLOs, cost per decision-grade observation, regional resilience.

Existing A10–A18 scale/persistence/provider-concurrency certification is useful engineering evidence, but live operational capacity must be measured with the actual Kidult data shape before claiming production scale.

## 10. Kidult 100 selection architecture
Candidate Universe 300–500
→ Data Sufficiency Gate
→ Rights/Provenance Gate
→ Factor Eligibility
→ Cross-category normalization
→ WHY evidence gate
→ Risk/confidence adjustment
→ Index Committee/Autonomous governed decision
→ Published 100
→ Watchlist / challengers
→ continuous re-evaluation.

No constituent may enter merely because data is plentiful. Selection is based on relevance, evidence quality, representativeness and future significance.

## 11. Initial factor architecture — research baseline, not frozen weights
- Canon / Cultural Persistence
- Collector Demand / Momentum
- Effective Scarcity
- Market Strength / Relative Value
- Liquidity
- Institutional Recognition
- Historical Persistence
- Future Canon Probability
- Risk / Confidence adjustment

Weights must be validated empirically and may differ by category. A single universal weight vector must not be assumed until backtested.

## 13. Non-goals
- collecting the entire internet;
- storing fields because they are easy to scrape;
- copying copyrighted descriptions/articles;
- using restricted sites merely because pages are publicly viewable;
- publishing synthetic market facts;
- confusing asking prices with transactions;
- ranking objects without explainability;
- maximizing record count at the expense of provenance and decision value.

## 14. Definition of leadership
KIDULTS leads the category when competitors can copy today's public facts but cannot reproduce:
- our historical observation series;
- canonical entity/comparable graph;
- rights-aware provenance chain;
- cross-category normalization;
- WHY/counter-evidence graph;
- confidence history;
- longitudinal scarcity/liquidity/demand/canon relationships;
- benchmark decision history;
- ability to explain what changed before consensus and what evidence would invalidate the thesis.

That compounding intelligence asset—not raw scraped data—is the moat.
