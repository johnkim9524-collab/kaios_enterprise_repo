# Intelligence Holdings Integrated Strategy V1

**Status:** MANDATORY STRATEGIC STEWARDSHIP  
**Owner:** KPMO  
**Applies to:** Intelligence Holdings and all verticals / brands  
**Principles:** Autonomous → Global → Irreplaceable Value → Transparent

## 1. Strategic objective

Intelligence Holdings must become a reusable global intelligence group whose durable value compounds in owned identity, evidence, methodology, decision memory, customer workflows and derived intelligence. External providers are inputs to the system, not the system itself.

The strategy stack is one integrated system: Product Strategy + Customer Strategy + Platform Strategy + Provider Strategy + Internalization Strategy + Go-to-Market/Monetization. A change in one layer must be evaluated for effects on the others.

## 2. Product strategy

Products must solve repeatable high-value decisions rather than sell undifferentiated records. The product ladder is:

1. Discovery and reference
2. Evidence-backed market intelligence
3. Rankability and decision support
4. Monitoring, workflow and alerts
5. Institutional data/API and enterprise intelligence

Every product must define target job-to-be-done, evidence standard, confidence/limitations, buyer, packaging, price logic, recurring-vs-transactional revenue, vertical differentiation, scale/kill criteria and cross-vertical reuse.

The product test: if a competitor can reproduce the core value by buying the same provider feed, it is not sufficiently differentiated.

## 3. Customer strategy

Prioritize customers by decision value, urgency, willingness to pay and evidence requirements, not by traffic alone.

Priority segments include serious collectors/HNW users; dealers, marketplaces and auctions; insurers, lenders, advisers and asset managers; brands/manufacturers/IP owners; research/media/data teams; institutional and enterprise buyers.

For each segment maintain a living contract for value proposition, critical decision, evidence/trust threshold, acquisition channel, activation event, willingness to pay, retention driver, expansion/cross-sell path, global language/currency/regional needs, enterprise security/governance needs and churn/non-usage root causes.

North star: trusted recurring decision utility and retention.

## 4. Platform strategy

Use one Intelligence Holdings shared control plane and intelligence fabric. Vertical duplication is an exception, not the default.

**Group-owned shared core:** canonical identity/entity resolution, normalization/ontology, rights and purpose control, evidence/provenance graph, methodology/confidence, decision memory, provider abstraction/switching, observability/ledger/recovery, AI governance, security/access control.

**Vertical-owned specialization:** ontology extensions, domain evidence rules, vertical products/workflows, brand experience, provider mix and vertical customer strategy.

A vertical may duplicate a group capability only with a documented measurable advantage, owner, expiry/exit criterion and migration path back to the shared core.

## 5. Provider strategy

Providers form a diversified evidence supply network. They must never become canonical truth or own the intelligence layer.

Evaluate every provider on rights/purpose fit, source authority, global coverage, freshness/reliability, incremental information value, field quality, cost/unit economics, quota constraints, lock-in, substitutability, derived-intelligence rights, provenance and auditability.

Classify providers by role: primary authoritative source, market transaction source, reference/catalog source, discovery source, certification/validation source, or derived/aggregated source. A provider can occupy more than one layer only when each use has independently verified rights and value.

Rules:

- No single provider is canonical truth.
- Material dependencies should have two substitution paths when feasible.
- Proof before procurement.
- Rights before recurring activation.
- Measure incremental value, not raw record count.
- Maintain one central provider registry with vertical usage mapping.
- Read back current communication history before outreach; duplicate contact is forbidden without authority.
- Every material provider requires an exit, retention/deletion and substitution plan.
- Shared provider dependencies must share health/quota/circuit state across producers.
- Code landing, CI success or readiness never equals provider activation authority.
- Optimize provider reuse and negotiated rights at Intelligence Holdings group level before vertical-specific duplicate purchasing.

Every material provider receives one of: `ACTIVATE_CANDIDATE`, `HOLD`, `REPLACE`, `DUAL_SOURCE`, `PARTNER`, `INTERNALIZE_DEPENDENCY`, or `SUNSET_PROVIDER`.

## 6. Internalization strategy

Internalize capabilities when ownership compounds differentiation, switching power, margin, learning or trust.

**Must-own candidates:** canonical identity/ER; normalization/ontology; provenance/evidence graph; rights decision memory; confidence/methodology; rankability/decision logic; provider routing/fusion/switching; data-quality controls; group intelligence ledger; customer/product learning loop; derived indices/signals/research; AI-agent governance and autonomous operations.

**Normally external:** source-originating data ownership, generic cloud infrastructure, payments/commodity infrastructure, third-party certification/official registry functions and replaceable commodity communications/observability components.

Internalization test:

1. Does ownership create compounding data or decision advantage?
2. Does it reduce provider lock-in?
3. Does it improve margin/unit economics at scale?
4. Does it protect proprietary method or learning?
5. Does it control a critical trust/provenance boundary?
6. Can it be reused across multiple verticals?

If three or more are materially true, KPMO must evaluate an owned-core path before long-term outsourcing is approved.

Internalization is selective ownership, not rebuilding everything. Commodity infrastructure should remain replaceable and external where economically rational.

Every internalization candidate receives one of: `INTERNALIZE_NOW`, `INTERNALIZE_WHEN_SCALE_TRIGGER_MET`, `HYBRID_OWNED_CORE_EXTERNAL_SOURCE`, `PARTNER_WITH_EXIT_PATH`, `KEEP_EXTERNAL_COMMODITY`, or `SUNSET_DEPENDENCY`.

## 7. Go-to-market and monetization

Design a deliberate free-to-paid path, self-serve/pro/enterprise tiers, trial/proof-of-value, regional pricing/currency, alerts/workspace/report/API packaging, vertical partnership channels, retention/expansion and sales-assist thresholds.

Do not infer product-market fit from traffic, provider volume, code completion or control tests. Market proof requires customer behavior and willingness-to-pay evidence.

## 8. Portfolio prioritization

Every material product/provider/platform initiative is scored on customer decision value, irreplaceability potential, global market size, rights feasibility, data availability, time-to-evidence, gross-margin potential, cross-vertical reuse, provider dependency risk and execution complexity.

Allowed portfolio states: `INVEST`, `PILOT`, `HOLD`, `PARTNER`, `INTERNALIZE`, `SUNSET`.

Every active initiative must have an owner, current evidence, next gate, kill criterion, economics view and strategic rationale.

## 9. Track ownership

- **KPMO:** integrated strategy accountability, portfolio conflict resolution, canonical strategic truth.
- **Track A:** product evidence feasibility, data-product input quality, source→product feedback.
- **Track B:** product-claim safety, decision-utility validation, methodology differentiation.
- **Track C:** customer experience, packaging surface, adoption/usage feedback.
- **Track D:** platform economics, scale/reliability, owned-core runtime feasibility.
- **Track E:** product/customer/platform portfolio strategy, IH/vertical future strategy, improvement prioritization.
- **Track Z:** provider portfolio, rights/economics, substitution and internalization inputs.

## 10. Continuous strategic loop

A material defect, customer signal, provider change, market change or new evidence must trigger not only remediation but a strategic-impact check.

`FIX ROOT CAUSE → VERIFY → TRUTH-SYNC → CURRENT IMPROVEMENT → PRODUCT/CUSTOMER/PLATFORM/PROVIDER/INTERNALIZATION IMPACT → GROUP/VERTICAL STRATEGY → PRIORITIZE`

Strategy must never delay an executable reversible internal defect correction.

## 11. Authority boundary

This strategy authorizes analysis, design, testing, prioritization and reversible internal preparation. It does not itself authorize provider activation, external communication, spend, contracts, credential expansion, irreversible legal/security change, Public, Production or G5.
